\title{An Analytic Origin for the Provenance Bias in Radial Migration: A Vertical
Form Factor, Corotation Trapping, and the Spiral $\JR$--$\hZ$ Relation}

\author{Carlos Vera-Ciro}
\affiliation{Independent Researcher}
\email{caverac@gmail.com}

\begin{abstract}
Radial migration acts preferentially on vertically cold stars, the ``provenance
bias'' of $N$-body disks \citep{VeraCiro2016imprint,Mikkola2020}. This paper derives it from first principles. A
star's coupling to a spiral is the arm's potential averaged over its vertical
oscillation, a form factor $F(\Jz,k)$ that is the spiral's vertical Fourier coefficient at
corotation, so vertically cold stars migrate preferentially.
Single-resonance trapping gives a weight $\propto F^{1/2}$ and floors the bias
near $-7$ percent, whereas resonance overlap, which the Milky Way bar and spiral reach at
physical spiral strengths, drives the disk into a diffusive regime in which the
angular-momentum diffusion coefficient scales as $F^2$. The dispersion offset of the
migrators then depends on how migrators are selected: the weight is $F$ for stars
weighted by $|\Delta\Lz|$, $F^2$ for $(\Delta\Lz)^2$, and $\mathrm{erfc}(\lambda/F)$ for
a threshold in $|\Delta\Lz|$. In a self-consistent $N$-body disk with recurrent
multi-arm spirals, over 0.5 to 2 Gyr, the variance of $\Delta\Lz$
grows as $F^{1.9}$ to $F^{2.0}$ on average, the dispersion ratio
per selection rule is reproduced to within 0.05, capture is symmetric in direction, and
inside 9 kpc the arrivals keep their vertical action, not their vertical energy.
The same disk thickness $\alpha\equiv k\hZ$ connects the bias to the spiral
$\JR^{\max}$--$\hZ$ relation \citep{Palicio2024}: the form factor selects the population
that defines $\JR^{\max}$, while the structural slope, set by the heating anisotropy
$(\sigR/\sigz)^2$, reproduces the measured $3.7\times10^{-2}\,\Lsun/\kpc$ and should
flatten with stellar age.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Transient, recurrent spiral arms
\citep{SellwoodCarlberg1984,DOnghia2013,Baba2013,SellwoodCarlberg2014} churn stellar
angular momentum at the corotation resonance, moving stars radially with little
heating \citep{SellwoodBinney2002,Roskar2008}. A robust but only qualitatively
understood feature of this process is its \emph{vertical selectivity}: in $N$-body
disks the stars that migrate are preferentially those on nearly circular orbits
confined to the plane.
\citet{VeraCiro2016imprint} named this the ``provenance bias'' and showed that it,
rather than vertical heating, sets the vertical structure of migrators: the
strongest migrators are preferentially the kinematically cool stars, those with
vertical velocity dispersions below the local mean (their Figure~4).
\citet{Mikkola2020} confirmed that the importance of migration at large vertical
action depends on spiral strength, and explicitly flagged an analytic description
as outstanding.

Independently, \citet{Palicio2024} report a linear relation, found across simulated
spiral galaxies and satisfied by the Milky Way, between the maximum radial action
of spiral-arm stars and the disk vertical scale $\hZ$, measured there as an
exponential vertical scale-length, $\JR^{\max}=a\,\hZ+b$ with
$a\approx3.7\times10^{-2}\,\Lsun/\kpc$ in units of the solar angular momentum
$\Lsun\equiv R_0V_0$ (see also \citealt{Jia2024}); the slope is not derived from first
principles.

This paper provides the missing dynamics. \citet{DanielWyse2015,DanielWyse2018}
derived an analytic corotation-capture criterion in which the maximum random energy
for capture scales with the spiral amplitude $|\Phi_s|$, so that low-dispersion
stars migrate preferentially, but their criterion is strictly in-plane. This paper
extends the problem to three dimensions and shows that the vertical structure enters
through a single object, the vertical form factor $F(\Jz,k)$
(Section~\ref{sec:formfactor}), identified as the exact vertical
Fourier coefficient of the spiral in the corotation-resonant Hamiltonian
(Section~\ref{sec:trapping}). Because $F$ sets the strength of that resonant
coupling, vertically cold stars are captured preferentially; single-resonance
trapping is too weak to explain the strong
cold-migrator preference of the simulations, and Section~\ref{sec:overlap} shows
that the Milky Way sits at the
\citet{Chirikov1979} resonance-overlap threshold, placing the churning in the
diffusive regime. Section~\ref{sec:bias} states the dispersion offset per selection
rule, with the Boltzmann capture weight as one closure, and derives the
$\JR^{\max}$--$\hZ$ slope, a structural ratio that the form factor enters only through
the population it selects, and its age dependence. The robust content of the model is
the scaling of the diffusion coefficient, $D_{\Lz}\propto F^2$; the size of a
dispersion offset follows from it only once the definition of a migrator is fixed.
Section~\ref{sec:nbody} tests both in a self-consistent $N$-body disk of $10^8$
particles with recurrent multi-arm spirals, where the exponent, the offsets per rule,
the symmetry of capture and the vertical state of the arrivals are measured directly,
and Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses the synthesis.

\section{The vertical form factor}
\label{sec:formfactor}

A razor-thin spiral perturbation of radial wavenumber $k$ has a potential that
satisfies Laplace's equation above the disk and therefore decays as $e^{-k|z|}$
\citep{BinneyTremaine2008}. A star executing a vertical oscillation
$z(\theta_z;\Jz)$ samples the spiral through the angle-average of that decay,
%
\begin{equation}
  F(\Jz,k) = \big\langle e^{-k|z(\theta_z,\Jz)|}\big\rangle_{\theta_z}
  = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^{z_m} \mathrm{d}z\, e^{-kz}\,\frac{\nu_z}{|v_z|},
  \label{eq:formfactor}
\end{equation}
%
with $z_m$ the turning point, $\nu_z$ the vertical frequency, $\Ez$ the conserved
vertical-oscillation energy and $v_z=\sqrt{2[\Ez-\Phi(z)]}$. Equation~\eqref{eq:formfactor}
is evaluated on the
self-consistent isothermal sheet \citep{Spitzer1942}, $\Phi(z)=2\ln\cosh z$ in
units $G=\sigz=z_0=1$, for which the only parameter is the dimensionless thickness
$\alpha\equiv kz_0$. By construction $F(0,k)=1$, since a mid-plane orbit feels the full
in-plane amplitude, and $F$ decreases monotonically with vertical action
(Figure~\ref{fig:formfactor}). Physically, $F$ is the average amplitude a star
feels: the time its orbit spends near the plane, weighted by the spiral's
$e^{-k|z|}$ reach. It makes precise the ``spend more time near the mid-plane'' half
of the verbal mechanism; that this orbit-average is itself the coupling strength
entering the corotation resonance is shown in Section~\ref{sec:trapping}.

For the Milky Way a radial wavelength $\lambda_R\sim3\kpc$
($k=2\pi/\lambda_R\sim2\kpc^{-1}$; \citealp{Reid2019}) and a vertical scale
$z_0\sim0.4\kpc$ \citep{BlandHawthornGerhard2016} give $\alpha\sim0.84$, of order
unity, so the vertical coupling is appreciably selective rather than negligible; the
resulting bias is quantified in the trapping and overlap analyses below.

  \caption{The vertical form factor $F(\Jz,k)=\langle e^{-k|z|}\rangle$
  (Equation~\eqref{eq:formfactor}) versus vertical action, for three wavenumbers
  $\alpha=kz_0$ ($\alpha=0.84$ approximates the Milky Way). $F$ is the orbit-averaged
  spiral amplitude a star feels: it equals unity for a mid-plane orbit by construction

  \label{fig:formfactor}

\paragraph{Finite-thickness correction.} The $e^{-k|z|}$ kernel is the razor-thin
leading term; the arm overdensity is in fact disk material of finite vertical extent
$h_s$ (comparable to the disk scale $z_0$), and convolving the thin-sheet kernel with
that profile rounds the mid-plane cusp \citep{Toomre1964}. For an exponential arm
profile $g(z)\propto e^{-|z|/h_s}$, with $h_s$ the arm's vertical scale height, the
softened kernel is $K(z)=[\beta e^{-k|z|}-k e^{-\beta|z|}]/(\beta-k)$ with
$\beta=1/h_s$, the thin-sheet kernel convolved with $g$ and normalized to $K(0)=1$,
a direct solution of Poisson's equation \citep{BinneyTremaine2008}. Its orbit average
is
%
\begin{equation}
  F_{\rm soft}(\Jz;\alpha,h)=\frac{F(\Jz,\alpha)-\alpha h\,F(\Jz,1/h)}{1-\alpha h},
  \, h\equiv h_s/z_0,
  \label{eq:softened}
\end{equation}
%
a combination of the bare form factor at the two wavenumbers (derived in
Appendix~\ref{app:softened}). It recovers $F$ as
$h\to0$ and tends to unity as $h\to\infty$. $F_{\rm soft}$ replaces the razor-thin $F$ when evaluating the Milky-Way
provenance bias in Section~\ref{sec:trapping}. The $N$-body disk of
Section~\ref{sec:nbody} shows that the correction is required rather than optional:
its arms have a $\mathrm{sech}^2$ vertical profile of scale $z_a\simeq0.8\,z_0$, and
only the form factor built on the potential of such a layer,
$\langle\phi_k(z)/\phi_k(0)\rangle$ with $\phi_k$ the convolution of
Appendix~\ref{app:softened} for the measured profile, gives the predicted scaling of
the angular-momentum diffusion, whereas the razor-thin kernel underestimates its
exponent by a factor of about 1.4 inside 8 kpc. The isothermal-sheet $F$ of
Equation~\eqref{eq:formfactor} is kept for the Milky Way estimates below, with
$F_{\rm soft}$ carrying the thickness.

\section{The corotation-resonant Hamiltonian and the trapped weight}
\label{sec:trapping}

This section shows that $F$ is not an inserted weight but the vertical structure factor
of corotation capture. Write the background in action-angle variables
$(\JR,\Lz,\Jz;\theta_R,\theta_\phi,\theta_z)$ with frequencies
$(\kappa,\Omega,\nu_z)$. A steady $m$-armed, tightly wound spiral of pattern speed
$\Omega_p$ is
%
\begin{equation}
  \Phi_s = \mathrm{Re}\!\left[\Phi_0(R)\,e^{-k|z|}\,
  e^{\,i(m\phi + \int\! \mathrm{d}R\, k - m\Omega_p t)}\right],
\end{equation}
%
whose Fourier expansion in the background angles,
%
\begin{equation}
  \Phi_s = \sum_{n_R,\,n_z} \Phi_{n_R n_z}(\JR,\Lz,\Jz)\,
  e^{\,i(n_R\theta_R + m\theta_\phi + n_z\theta_z - m\Omega_p t)},
\end{equation}
%
has coefficients that factorize into independent radial and vertical fast-angle
averages; here $n_R$ and $n_z$ are the integer harmonics of the radial and vertical
angles (the azimuthal harmonic is fixed at the arm number $m$). The $(n_R,n_z)$ term
is resonant when its phase is stationary, $n_R\kappa + n_z\nu_z + m(\Omega-\Omega_p)=0$.
At corotation, $\Omega(\Lz)=\Omega_p$, this is the $n_R=n_z=0$ harmonic, the part of
the spiral averaged over both the radial epicycle and the vertical orbit, so the
resonant coupling is
%
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \Psi(\JR,\Jz) &= \big\langle \Phi_s \big\rangle_{\theta_R,\theta_z} \\
                &= \Phi_0(R_{\rm CR})\,J_0(k a_R)\,F(\Jz,k),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:coupling}
\end{equation}
%
the spiral averaged over the fast angles $\theta_R,\theta_z$ and evaluated at the
corotation radius $R_{\rm CR}$ (where $\Omega=\Omega_p$), with epicyclic amplitude
$a_R=\sqrt{2\JR/\kappa}$. The coupling factorizes into the classical radial reduction
factor $G(\JR)\equiv J_0(ka_R)$
\citep[the Kalnajs/Bessel factor;][]{BinneyTremaine2008} and the vertical form
factor $F$. This coupling $\Psi$ is the amplitude of the corotation resonance, the
depth of the pendulum well that traps migrating stars
(Equations~\eqref{eq:pendulum}--\eqref{eq:width} below), so its vertical factor $F$
directly sets how strongly a star of given $\Jz$ is captured and migrates.
\emph{The provenance bias is the $F$-factor of this coupling; the
\citet{DanielWyse2015} radial selectivity is the $G$-factor.} Equation~\eqref{eq:coupling} is the central identification of this paper: $F(\Jz,k)$ is the
exact vertical Fourier coefficient of the spiral at corotation.

Transforming to the rotating frame (the Jacobi integral $H_J=H_0-\Omega_p\Lz+
\Phi_s$ is conserved) and averaging over the fast angles $\theta_R,\theta_z$, for
which $\JR$ and $\Jz$ are adiabatic invariants, leaves the slow Hamiltonian. The
invariance is approximate rather than exact for an individual star in a
time-dependent disk, and holds in the ensemble sense: \citet{Minchev2012thickening}
found the vertical action conserved on average, not star by star, as stellar samples
change guiding radius, and \citet{VeraCiro2016conservation} measured, in the
\texttt{LD} disk used in Section~\ref{sec:nbody}, an average fractional change below 1
percent even after 40 orbital periods, with a dispersion of the fractional changes of
20 to 28 percent over 2 Gyr that grows to 50 percent after 6 Gyr; the migrators of
Section~\ref{sec:nbody} change their $\Jz$ by 0 to 16 percent in the median over 2
Gyr. The reduction below holds in that sense. Expanding
about corotation in $p\equiv\Lz-L_{\rm CR}$, where $\mathrm{d}H_0/\mathrm{d}\Lz=
\Omega-\Omega_p$ vanishes, gives a pendulum
%
\begin{equation}
  \bar H(p,\theta_r) = \tfrac{1}{2}\,g\,p^2 + \Psi\cos\theta_r,
  \qquad g\equiv\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}\Omega}{\mathrm{d}\Lz}\right|_{\rm CR},
  \label{eq:pendulum}
\end{equation}
%
following the action-angle resonant-trapping construction of \citet{Monari2017},
here extended to carry the vertical degree of freedom through $\Psi$. The libration
(trapped, migrating) island has half-width in $p$
%
\begin{equation}
  \Delta p = 2\sqrt{\Psi/|g|} \;\propto\; \sqrt{\,F(\Jz,k)\,},
  \label{eq:width}
\end{equation}
%
so vertically cold stars (large $F$) sit in wider libration islands and are trapped
preferentially. Counting the trapped phase-space area gives a single-resonance
migration weight
%
\begin{equation}
  W(\Jz) \;\propto\; F(\Jz,k)^{1/2},
  \label{eq:wsqrtf}
\end{equation}
%
the island-area-counting law. The \emph{exact} trapped fraction follows from
integrating a Gaussian parent distribution of the slow action $p$, of dispersion
$\sigma_p$, over the separatrix (Appendix~\ref{app:trapped}):
%
\begin{equation}
  W(\Jz) = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\pi \mathrm{d}u\,
  \mathrm{erf}\!\left(\frac{\xi\sqrt{F(\Jz,k)}}{\sqrt2}\,\sin u\right),
  \label{eq:wexact}
\end{equation}
%
with a single dimensionless island-width parameter
$\xi\equiv p_{\max}(F{=}1)/\sigma_p\propto\sqrt{|\Phi_s|}$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:wexact} reduces to the $\sqrt{F}$ law~\eqref{eq:wsqrtf} for
narrow islands ($\xi\to0$) and saturates to unity for wide ones
(Figure~\ref{fig:trappedweight}). The provenance bias follows by reweighting the
equilibrium vertical distribution $\mathrm{d}N/\mathrm{d}\Ez\propto e^{-\Ez}/\nu_z$ by the
trapped weight $W(\Jz)$ and forming the migrator-to-parent dispersion ratio
$\sigz^{\rm mig}/\sigz^{\rm all}$; Figure~\ref{fig:trappingcap} plots the resulting
log dispersion ratio $\ln\sigz^{\rm mig}/\sigz^{\rm all}$ (negative; more negative is
stronger) at the Milky-Way thickness against the
island-width $\xi$. It is \emph{most negative} in the dilute limit, where
$W\propto\sqrt{F}$ gives the steepest vertical weighting, and weakens (toward zero)
monotonically with $\xi$: the exact trapped census therefore \emph{floors} the bias
at the $\sqrt{F}$ value, ${\approx}-7$ percent at the Milky-Way thickness, for any
spiral strength.
Finite spiral thickness softens this floor further: replacing $F$ by the
finite-thickness $F_{\rm soft}$~\eqref{eq:softened} weakens the ${\approx}-7$ percent
floor to ${\approx}-4$ to $-5.5$ percent across the plausible Milky-Way spiral thickness
$h\sim0.5$--$1$ (Figure~\ref{fig:thickness}), a $20$--$45$ percent reduction in magnitude.
Even the steeper \citet{DanielWyse2018} capture-fraction accounting, $W\propto F$,
reaches only ${\approx}-14$ percent. Neither single-resonance estimate produces the
strong cold-migrator preference seen in the simulations, in which the migrators are
drawn overwhelmingly from the kinematically cool population
\citep{VeraCiro2016imprint}; that preference points to the resonance-overlap regime
of Section~\ref{sec:overlap}, where $W$ steepens toward the diffusive $F^2$ limit. The effective weight $W=\exp[-s(1/F-1)]$
derived for the bias in Section~\ref{sec:bias}, Equation~\eqref{eq:effweight}, is
accordingly a \emph{diffusive} effective model, not the single-resonance census.

\section{Resonance overlap and the diffusive regime}
\label{sec:overlap}

The single-resonance estimates of Section~\ref{sec:trapping}, the exact trapped
census ($\sqrt{F}$, ${\approx}-7$ percent; Figure~\ref{fig:trappingcap}) and the
\citet{DanielWyse2018} capture fraction ($\propto F$, ${\approx}-14$ percent),
are both too weak to produce the strong cold-migrator preference of the
simulations. Single isolated corotation trapping is therefore not the operative
regime. Whether the Milky Way is
instead in the resonance-overlap (stochastic, diffusive) regime is tested with the
\citet{Chirikov1979} criterion.

For a flat rotation curve ($\Omega=V_c/R$, $|g|=1/R^2$) the corotation libration
half-width~\eqref{eq:width} is a fixed fraction of the corotation radius,
%
\begin{equation}
  \Delta R / R = 2\sqrt{\epsilon\,G\,F},
  \qquad \epsilon \equiv |\Phi_s|/V_c^2 ,
\end{equation}
%
and two patterns with corotation radii $R_{\rm in}<R_{\rm out}$ overlap when the sum
of their island half-widths exceeds their separation,
%
\begin{equation}
  S \equiv \frac{\Delta R_{\rm in}+\Delta R_{\rm out}}{R_{\rm out}-R_{\rm in}}
  = 2\sqrt{\epsilon\,G\,F}\,\frac{\Omega_{\rm in}+\Omega_{\rm out}}
                                  {\Omega_{\rm in}-\Omega_{\rm out}} \ge 1 .
  \label{eq:chirikov}
\end{equation}
%
For the Milky Way bar ($\Omega_p\approx38\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,kpc^{-1}}$,
$R_{\rm CR}\approx6.0\kpc$) and spiral
($\Omega_p\approx20\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,kpc^{-1}}$, $R_{\rm CR}\approx11.5\kpc$),
Equation~\eqref{eq:chirikov} gives $S=0.64$, $0.91$, $1.12$ at $\epsilon=0.01$,
$0.02$, $0.03$, so at the physical spiral strength ($|\Phi_s|\sim1$--$3\%\,V_c^2$)
the bar and spiral corotation resonances sit at the overlap threshold $S\sim1$, the
mechanism of \citet{Minchev2010}. \citet{Quillen2003} first applied the overlap
argument to the Galactic bar and spiral, for the spiral's inner Lindblad resonance
against the bar's outer Lindblad resonance near the Sun, a different resonance pair
from the corotation pair estimated here. The threshold conclusion depends on the two
pattern speeds through the corotation radii, and Table~\ref{tab:overlap} gives $S$ at
$\epsilon=0.02$ across the literature range. For the mainstream values of the bar, 33
to 41 km/s/kpc \citep{ClarkeGerhard2022,Binney2020,Portail2017,Sanders2019,Bovy2019},
$S$ runs from 1.13 to 0.82, so the Milky Way stays at the threshold, and the slow bar of
\citet{Horta2025}, 24 km/s/kpc, places it well inside the overlap regime. The spiral
pattern speed matters as much: with the bar at 38 km/s/kpc, $S=0.78$ for the 17.8
km/s/kpc of the Perseus arm \citep{CastroGinard2021} and 1.91 for the 28.2 km/s/kpc of
\citet{Dias2019}. No plausible pair of pattern speeds puts $S$ far below unity at the
physical strengths. A bar is not required: in an unbarred disk the same overlap arises
among co-existing transient patterns of different pattern speed
\citep{SellwoodCarlberg2014}, and the disk of Section~\ref{sec:nbody}, which has no bar,
carries several corotating patterns per harmonic at once.

Crucially, since $\Delta R\propto\sqrt{F}$, the overlap parameter scales as
$S\propto\sqrt{F}$: vertically cold stars cross the threshold first
(Figure~\ref{fig:overlap}). At the fiducial parameters the cold ($F=1$) population
overlaps at $\epsilon\approx0.024$, inside the physical band, while the hot
($F=0.5$) population stays in regular libration until $\epsilon\approx0.05$. The
diffusive churning channel is therefore itself biased toward vertically cold stars,
steepening the provenance bias beyond the single-resonance $F^{1/2}$ law toward the
$F^2$ diffusive limit. The Milky Way spiral sector lives at the crossover between
regular trapping and overlap diffusion, and that crossover is vertical-action
selective.

  Overlap, the onset of diffusive churning, sets in at $S=1$, reached within the
  physical strength band (shaded); and because $S\propto\sqrt{F}$, cold stars cross
  first, so in that band only the cold population is diffusive while hot stars stay
  trapped. Overlapping resonances are the resolution to the single-resonance shortfall,
  and the transition is itself cold-selective.}
  \label{fig:overlap}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The crossover weight.} The two regimes are limits of one migration
weight. Quasilinear churning diffusion gives an angular-momentum diffusion
coefficient $D\propto|\Psi|^2\propto F^2$, so the diffusive weight is $W\propto F^2$
where resonances overlap, while the trapped census gives $W\propto F^{1/2}$ where
they do not. Modeling the weight as a power of the form factor whose exponent runs
from the trapping value $1/2$ to the diffusive value $2$ through a gate centered
on the threshold $S=1$,
%
\begin{equation}
  W(\Jz)=F^{p},\;\; p=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{2}\frac{S^2}{1+S^2},\;\;
  \frac{S(\Jz)}{S_0}=\sqrt{F(\Jz,k)},
  \label{eq:crossover}
\end{equation}
%
with the single physical parameter $S_0$ the effective overlap, the predicted bias
deepens monotonically from the $\sqrt{F}$ value (${\approx}-7$ percent) to the $F^2$
value (${\approx}-27$ percent) as $S_0$ grows (Figure~\ref{fig:crossover}). The bar
and spiral alone ($S_0\approx1$) give ${\approx}-11$ percent; the diffusive band
(${\approx}-20$ to $-27$ percent) is reached at $S_0\approx2$--$3$, the extra overlap
expected once the Milky Way's recurrent transient spirals are included. The crossover
sits at the critical form factor $F_{\rm crit}=1/S_0^2$, a critical vertical
action separating diffusive (cold) from confined (hot) migrators, a structural
prediction. The endpoints are physical; the gate is the minimal model of the
crossover sharpness.

  \caption{The provenance bias $\ln\sigz^{\rm mig}/\sigz^{\rm all}$ at the Milky-Way
  thickness from the crossover weight~\eqref{eq:crossover}, versus the effective
  resonance overlap $S_0$. As $S_0$ grows the weight steepens from $\sqrt{F}$ (trapping)
  toward $F^2$ (diffusion), and the bias deepens from the ${\approx}-7$ percent floor to
  the ${\approx}-27$ percent $F^2$ value; the bar-plus-spiral overlap alone (shaded,
  $S_0\approx1$) already gives ${\approx}-11$ percent. Overlap deepens the bias well
  past the single-resonance floor, further still as the Milky Way's recurrent transient
  arms raise $S_0$.}
  \label{fig:crossover}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{First-principles diffusive weight.} The diffusive endpoint is not assumed
but follows from kinetic theory. The inhomogeneous Balescu--Lenard corotation
diffusion coefficient in angular momentum,
$D_{\Lz}(J)=\pi\sum_m|\Psi_m(J)|^2 C_m(\Omega)$
\citep{Heyvaerts2010,Fouvry2015}, inherits the
$|\Psi_m|^2\propto F^2$ of the resonant coupling~\eqref{eq:coupling}, so the diffusive
migration weight is $W\propto F^2$. A finite libration width
$\Delta\omega\propto\sqrt{\Psi}\propto\sqrt{F}$ broadens the resonant denominator
\citep{Dupree1966}; for a Lorentzian spiral spectrum of width $W_s$ this yields the
closed form
%
\begin{equation}
  W(\Jz)=\frac{F^2}{1+b\sqrt{F}},\qquad b=\frac{\Delta\omega}{W_s},
  \label{eq:bl}
\end{equation}
%
interpolating the decorrelated quasilinear limit ($W\propto F^2$, $b\to0$) and the
coherent, resonance-broadened limit ($W\propto F^{3/2}$, $b\to\infty$). The provenance
bias in this diffusive regime is therefore confined to a narrow \emph{derived} band,
${\approx}-20$ to $-27$ percent at the Galactic thickness, almost independent of $b$
(Figure~\ref{fig:bl}): the observed bias follows from the disk being in the diffusive
(overlap) regime, with no tuned parameter. The crossover weight~\eqref{eq:crossover}
thus bridges the derived $\sqrt{F}$ value (${\approx}-7$ percent) and this derived
diffusive band.

\begin{figure}
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{balescu-lenard.pdf}
  \caption{The provenance bias $\ln\sigz^{\rm mig}/\sigz^{\rm all}$ at the Milky-Way
  thickness from the first-principles Balescu--Lenard diffusive weight~\eqref{eq:bl},
  versus the resonance-broadening parameter $b$. It is pinned to the narrow band
  between its $F^2$ and $F^{3/2}$ limits (shaded), ${\approx}-20$ to $-27$ percent,
  nearly independent of $b$. The diffusive bias is thus fixed with no free parameter:
  its size follows from the disk simply being in the overlap regime.}
  \label{fig:bl}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Tests.} The mechanism is tested in Section~\ref{sec:nbody}, first by
direct orbit integration in an imposed spiral and then in a self-consistent $N$-body
disk.

\section{The provenance bias and the \texorpdfstring{$\JR^{\max}$--$\hZ$}{JRmax-hZ} relation}
\label{sec:bias}

\paragraph{Bias magnitude.} In the diffusive regime a star is captured into
migration with a probability set by the competition between its radial random energy
$\sigR^2$ and the resonant coupling energy $|\Phi_s|\,F$, the spiral amplitude
reduced by the vertical form factor of Equation~\eqref{eq:coupling}. A Boltzmann capture
probability $\propto\exp(-\sigR^2/|\Phi_s|F)$, normalized to a mid-plane orbit
($F=1$), gives the effective migration weight
%
\begin{equation}
  W(\Jz)=\exp\!\left[-s\left(\frac{1}{F(\Jz,k)}-1\right)\right],
  \qquad s\equiv\frac{\sigR^2}{|\Phi_s|},
  \label{eq:effweight}
\end{equation}
%
the smooth, three-dimensional form of the \citet{DanielWyse2015} random-energy
capture criterion. Applying the reweighting of Section~\ref{sec:trapping} with this
$W$ yields the migrator dispersion ratio $\sigz^{\rm mig}/\sigz^{\rm all}$. At the Milky-Way thickness $\alpha=0.84$ the
predicted bias spans ${\approx}-20$ to $-41$ percent across the physical range
$s\sim0.8$--$2$ ($|\Phi_s|\sim1$--$3\%\,V_c^2$), covering the derived diffusive band
(${\approx}-20$ to $-27$ percent; Section~\ref{sec:overlap}) with no tuning; the model predicts
the bias weakens as the spiral strengthens and strengthens with disk thickness
$\alpha$ (Figure~\ref{fig:bias}), matching the strength dependence of
\citet{Mikkola2020} and the morphology independence of \citet{VeraCiro2016imprint}. As
Section~\ref{sec:overlap} makes explicit, the steepness encoded in $s$ is the
signature of the diffusive (overlap) regime rather than of clean single-resonance
trapping: this effective weight is the phenomenological counterpart of the crossover
weight~\eqref{eq:crossover} at $S_0\approx2$--$3$. Equation~\eqref{eq:effweight} is a
closure, not a consequence of the resonant Hamiltonian: it converts the coupling into
a capture probability through the one parameter $s$, and the $-20$ to $-41$ percent
band is its range over $s$. What the Hamiltonian fixes is the diffusion coefficient
$D_{\Lz}\propto F^2$, and the dispersion offset of a migrator population follows from
it only together with the rule that defines a migrator. A star that diffuses with
that coefficient for a time $\Delta t$ has a change $\Delta\Lz$ of variance
$V_1 F^2$, with $V_1$ the variance at $F=1$, so a population weighted by
$|\Delta\Lz|$ has the weight $F$, one weighted by $(\Delta\Lz)^2$ the weight $F^2$, and
the stars with $|\Delta\Lz|$ above a threshold $L_*$ the weight
$\mathrm{erfc}(\lambda/F)$ with $\lambda=L_*/\sqrt{2V_1}$. At $\alpha=0.84$ the
first two give offsets of about $-14$ and $-27$ percent, the values of the $F$ and
$F^2$ weights above, and the threshold rule is steeper than $F^2$ for a high
percentile. Section~\ref{sec:nbody} measures all three in a self-consistent disk.

  disk thickness $\alpha=k\hZ$, from the diffusive capture weight~\eqref{eq:effweight},
  banded over the physical spiral-strength range $s=0.8$--$2$
  ($|\Phi_s|\sim1$--$3\%\,V_c^2$; solid $s=0.8$, dashed $s=2$). The bias deepens with

\paragraph{Structural slope.} Combining the isothermal-sheet scale height
$\hZ=\sigz^2/(\pi G\Sigma)$ with the epicyclic action
$\JR^{\max}=c_{\max}\sigR^2/\kappa$ gives the slope as the squared
radial-to-vertical heating-anisotropy ratio,
%
\begin{equation}
  a \;=\; \frac{\JR^{\max}}{\hZ}
    \;=\; c_{\max}\,\frac{\pi G\Sigma}{\kappa}\left(\frac{\sigR}{\sigz}\right)^2 .
  \label{eq:slope}
\end{equation}
%
For solar-neighborhood values ($\Sigma=50\,M_\odot\,\mathrm{pc}^{-2}$,
$\kappa=0.037\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,pc^{-1}}$, $\sigR=35$, $\sigz=18\,
\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$) and $c_{\max}=1$, Equation~\eqref{eq:slope} gives
$3.6\times10^{-2}\,\Lsun/\kpc$, within a few percent of the measured
$3.7\times10^{-2}$. Equation~\eqref{eq:slope} uses the self-gravitating
$\mathrm{sech}^2$ sheet, whereas \citet{Palicio2024} fit $\hZ$ as an exponential
vertical scale-length; the two scales differ by an order-unity factor, which is
absorbed into $c_{\max}$.

\paragraph{Age-running.} Because $\Sigma$ and $\kappa$ are structural, the slope's
only population dependence is $(\sigR/\sigz)^2$. Each channel obeys an
age--velocity-dispersion relation $\sigma\propto\mathrm{age}^\beta$, so
%
\begin{equation}
  a(\mathrm{age}) \;\propto\; \mathrm{age}^{\,2(\beta_R-\beta_z)} .
  \label{eq:agerun}
\end{equation}
%
With $\beta_z\approx0.5$ and $\beta_R\approx0.35$
\citep{AumerBinneySchonrich2016,Sun2024}, the exponent is
$-0.3$, so the slope of an $8\,$Gyr population is $\approx0.54$ times that of a
$1\,$Gyr population: \emph{the relation should flatten with age}. Re-fitting the
$\JR^{\max}$--$\hZ$ relation in mono-age or mono-abundance bins thus measures
$\beta_R-\beta_z$ from spiral-arm kinematics; an age-independent slope would instead
require $\beta_R=\beta_z$.

\section{Test in a self-consistent \texorpdfstring{$N$}{N}-body disk}
\label{sec:nbody}

The predictions of Sections~\ref{sec:trapping} to \ref{sec:bias} are tested in two
steps: first by direct orbit integration in an imposed spiral, which checks the
mechanism with no analytic input but shares the model's assumptions of a fixed
background and a razor-thin arm, and then in a self-consistent $N$-body disk, which
shares none of them.

\paragraph{Test particles.} Integrating ${\sim}3000$ test particles in
\texttt{MWPotential2014} \citep{Bovy2015} plus an imposed transient spiral that falls
off vertically over a scale height $H$, and weighting each star by its churning
$|\Delta\Lz|$, recovers the provenance bias (Figure~\ref{fig:testparticle}). Here $H$
is the spiral's vertical scale, the simulation's stand-in for the analytic reach
$1/k$ of the $e^{-k|z|}$ fall-off and distinct from the disk scale height $z_0=\hZ$,
so the simulation's dimensionless thinness $\alpha\equiv z_0/H$ is the counterpart
of the analytic $\alpha=kz_0$; the correspondence between the two is only approximate,
since the galpy vertical profile is a $\mathrm{sech}$-type form rather than a pure
$e^{-k|z|}$, although the ratio $z_0/H$ itself is exact. Migrators are vertically
colder, and the bias strengthens with $\alpha$ in agreement with the single-resonance
prediction, the log dispersion ratio reaching
$\ln\sigz^{\rm mig}/\sigz^{\rm all}\approx-0.06$ (migrators ${\approx}6$ percent colder)
near the Galactic $\alpha\sim1$. Stacking $M$ overlapping patterns (the pattern
count, not the arm number $m$; each keeps the same $m$ and hence $k$ at a different
pattern speed) then deepens the bias monotonically, confirming the
resonance-overlap mechanism of Section~\ref{sec:overlap}. The same panel carries
the origin rings of the $N$-body disk described below, under the same
$|\Delta\Lz|$ weight and placed at $\alpha=kz_0$ with the ring's arm wavenumber:
they lie between the single-resonance $\sqrt F$ curve and the razor-thin diffusive
$F$ curve, and on the diffusive $F$ curve computed with the softened form factor of
an arm 0.8 disk scale heights thick, the thickness measured in that disk. The two
tests and the analytic weights are thus read in one variable, and the self-consistent
disk sits where the diffusive regime with a thick arm puts it.

\paragraph{The simulation.} The simulation is the \texttt{LD} (``light disk'') model of
\citet{VeraCiro2016imprint}, the disk introduced by \citet{DOnghia2013} and analyzed
by \citet{VeraCiro2014}, in its $10^8$-particle realization: an exponential disk of
$M_d=1.81\times10^{10}\,M_\odot$ and $R_d=3.13\kpc$ with a $\mathrm{sech}^2$ profile of
$z_0=0.1R_d=0.313\kpc$, in a rigid Hernquist halo of $9.5\times10^{11}\,M_\odot$ and
scale radius $29.8\kpc$, with 1,000 perturbers of $9.5\times10^5\,M_\odot$ on circular
orbits that seed the arms. The same disk with the perturbers removed after 245 Myr was followed for
5.5 Gyr by \citet{VeraCiro2014}, who found the spread of origin radii growing as
$t^{1/2}$, the signature of the diffusive regime of Section~\ref{sec:overlap}; it was
followed for 6 Gyr by \citet{VeraCiro2016conservation}, who measured the conservation
of $\Jz$ quoted in Section~\ref{sec:trapping}, and to 9.8 Gyr by
\citet{VeraCiro2016imprint}, by which time its radial dispersion had grown by 30
percent. The arms are a set of
local corotating features rather than one global mode: between $t=0.49$ and 0.78 Gyr,
the origin window of the test, the harmonics $m=5$ to 8 dominate with density
amplitudes $\delta\Sigma/\Sigma=9$ to 13 percent at 5 to 8 kpc, pitch angles of 20 to
40 degrees and radial wavenumbers $k=1.4$ to $2.5\kpc^{-1}$, each with several
spectrogram peaks whose corotation lies within 1 kpc of the peak radius; the amplitudes
decline to 5 to 8 percent by $t=2.5$ Gyr while the wavenumbers drift by 20 percent. A
control run of the same disk without perturbers bounds what the disk does to itself:
its variance of $\Delta\Lz$ is 0.4 to 0.8 percent of the perturbed run's in every ring
from 3 to 12 kpc.

\paragraph{The measurement.} From 611 snapshots a random $10^6$ disk particles are
followed as tracks, and the azimuthal harmonics $m=1$ to 8 of all $10^8$ particles are
summed per snapshot in rings of 0.25 kpc, which give the arms' amplitudes and
wavenumbers. Over a window of 293 Myr each tracked star gets its mean $\Lz$, the actions
$\Jz=(2\pi)^{-1}\oint v_z\,\mathrm{d}z$ and $\JR=(2\pi)^{-1}\oint v_R\,\mathrm{d}R$
as loop integrals between the first and the last upward crossing, the periods, and the
form factor along the orbit, $F=\langle\phi_k(z)/\phi_k(0)\rangle$, at the wavenumber
of its origin ring (the mean over $m=5$ to 8 with the squared amplitudes as weights).
The form factor is therefore measured on the simulation's own orbits, not modeled;
Equation~\eqref{eq:formfactor} enters only through the kernel. Each star gets an
origin ring of 1 kpc, from 3 to 12 kpc, from the guiding radius of its mean $\Lz$, and
$\Delta\Lz$ is the change of its mean $\Lz$ to a later window at a baseline of 0.49,
0.98 or 1.96 Gyr. In the origin window 85 percent of the stars complete a vertical
period (median 82 Myr) and 72 percent a radial one (median 97 Myr).

\paragraph{The variance of $\Delta\Lz$.} In the diffusive regime $D_{\Lz}\propto F^2$,
so at fixed origin ring $\mathrm{Var}(\Delta\Lz)\propto F^2$ with no free parameter.
$\mathrm{Var}(\Delta\Lz)\propto F^{b}$ is fitted in eight quantile bins of $F$ per ring
(Figure~\ref{fig:nbodyvar}). With the razor-thin $F=\langle e^{-k|z|}\rangle$ the
exponent is 0.9 to 1.35 inside 8 kpc and 1.8 to 3.2 outside (circles in
Figure~\ref{fig:nbodyexp}), and the rings collapse no better on $F$ than on
$\langle|z|\rangle$. A sweep of the wavenumber of $F$ explains the shortfall: in each
ring the product $b_k k$ is nearly constant while $b_k$ falls by a factor of 6 from
$k=0.5$ to $4\kpc^{-1}$, so the variance follows $\exp(-2k_{\rm eff}\langle|z|\rangle)$
with an effective wavenumber about half the arm's inside 8 kpc, and the local slopes
flatten toward small $|z|$. Both are what the potential of a thick arm produces. The
arms' vertical profile, measured by projecting the harmonics of the particles in bins
of $|z|$ on the phase of the whole ring, falls for $m=7$ at 5 to 8 kpc from 11 to 12
percent at $|z|<0.1\kpc$ to 5 to 7 percent at 0.45 to 0.65 kpc and 2 to 4 percent at
0.65 to 1 kpc, with the arm at height lagging the midplane arm by up to 50 degrees,
and its second moment gives a $\mathrm{sech}^2$ scale $z_a=0.25\kpc$, 0.8 of the
disk's, for every harmonic inside 10 kpc. With the layer form factor, the orbit average
of $\phi_k(z)/\phi_k(0)$ for the convolution of Appendix~\ref{app:softened} with this
profile, the exponents become 1.4 to 1.9 inside 8 kpc and 2.0 to 2.8 outside, with
means of 1.98, 1.98 and 1.88 over the nine rings at the three baselines
(Figure~\ref{fig:nbodyexp}), and the rings collapse on $F$ to 0.065 to 0.085 dex rms
against 0.13 to 0.14 on $\langle|z|\rangle$: the dependence is on $kz$, not on $z$. The
razor-thin kernel fails because its cusp at $z=0$ makes $F$ vary too fast across the
heights that most stars reach, which are all inside the arm. Figure~\ref{fig:nbodyexp}
collects the exponents per ring: with the layer form factor the three baselines agree
within the ring-to-ring scatter, so the exponent is a property of the disk and not of
the interval, and the razor-thin circles fall below the lines inside 8 kpc by the
factor of 1.4 that the thickness of the arms accounts for. Its lower panel shows the
variance of $\Delta\Lz$ in units of $L_c^2$, the squared angular momentum of the
circular orbit at the ring. At fixed ring the variance grows in proportion to the
baseline inside 9 kpc, by a factor of 3.4 to 4.1 over the fourfold interval, the
signature of a random walk, and faster outside, by a factor of 5 to 6, where the
arrivals are also found to be heated below. Across rings the relative spread falls by
a factor of 20 to 40 from 3 to 12 kpc, because $L_c$ grows with radius while the
amplitude of the arms peaks at 5 to 8 kpc: after 1.96 Gyr a star at 3 to 4 kpc has
changed its angular momentum by 33 percent of $L_c$ in the root mean square, a star at
11 to 12 kpc by 7 percent. The excess kurtosis of $\Delta\Lz$ is 0.2 to 1.7 inside 10
kpc, while the migrators keep their radial action inside 8 kpc (median relative change
of $\JR$ of 0.22 to 0.51 against 0.29 to 0.53 for the rest), which separates corotation
churning from direct scattering by the perturbers.

\paragraph{The bias per selection rule.} In each origin ring the measured
$\sigz^{\rm mig}/\sigz^{\rm all}$ for the three rules of Section~\ref{sec:bias} is
compared with the ratio obtained by reweighting the same stars with $F$, $F^2$ and
$\mathrm{erfc}(\lambda/F)$, with $L_*$ the 90th percentile of $|\Delta\Lz|$ in the ring
(Table~\ref{tab:rules}). Figure~\ref{fig:nbodyrules} shows the comparison ring by ring
at the 1.96 Gyr baseline, and two features of it carry the result. First, the rules
are separated by 0.05 to 0.09 in the ratio in every ring, the threshold selecting the coldest
stars and the linear weight the least cold, and the model reproduces the separation
and the ordering with no parameter beyond the exponent 2 of the diffusive regime; the
trapping weight $\sqrt F$ of a single resonance would give 0.96 to 0.97 in every ring,
above the measured ratio of every rule.
Second, the difference between the model and the measured ratio is within 0.05 for
every rule, ring and baseline, with a sign that follows the exponent of
Figure~\ref{fig:nbodyexp}: inside 8 kpc, where $b<2$, the model ratio is too low by
0.01 to 0.05, that is, the model is too strong, since a weight built on $F^2$
overstates the contrast between cold and hot stars where the variance grows more
slowly than $F^2$, whereas at 8 to 10 kpc, where $b>2$, it is too high by 0.01 to 0.04.
The offset in this disk is therefore 5 to 9, 10 to 16 or 16 to 22 percent according to
the rule, which is why it is quoted per rule rather than as one number, and why the
closure of Equation~\eqref{eq:effweight} is not to be read as a measurement.

\paragraph{Inward and outward migrators.} Because the weight depends on $\Jz$ only,
the capture is symmetric in the sign of $\Delta\Lz$: the inward and the outward
migrators of an origin ring have the same $\Jz$ distribution (Kolmogorov-Smirnov
distance 0.02 to 0.12 in every ring and baseline), both at 0.55 to 0.8 of the ring's
median. The destination state is not symmetric, and Figure~\ref{fig:nbodydest} shows
which conservation law it follows. At the ring where they arrive, inward migrators are
24 to 29 percent colder than the stars already there and outward migrators 6 to 12
percent colder. The prediction with $\Jz$ conserved,
$\langle v_z^2\rangle=\Jz\nu_z$ with the arrival's own vertical period at the
destination, runs through the measurements inside 9 kpc within 0.015 to 0.07 in both
directions, whereas conservation of the vertical energy sits 0.11 to 0.15 below the
inward measurements and above unity for the outward ones, that is, it makes outward
migrators hotter than the locals, which the simulation rules out.
Outside 9 kpc the arrivals are hotter than the $\Jz$ prediction by a gap that grows
with the baseline, from 0.06 to 0.11 at 0.49 Gyr to 0.08 to 0.15 at 1.96 Gyr. That is
the part of the disk where the arms are weakest, 5 to 7 percent for the dominant
harmonic against 12 percent at 5 to 8 kpc, where the variance of $\Delta\Lz$ grows
faster than the baseline (Figure~\ref{fig:nbodyexp}), as it would if later arms kept
reaching those rings, and where the migrators heat radially as well, with a median
relative change of $\JR$ of 2.4 against 0.2 for the rest at 11 to 12 kpc; the outer
disk is not in the single random-walk regime of the model, and the quantitative claims
of this paper stop at 9 kpc.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The form factor is measured, so the test checks the
exponent and the reweighting, not the isothermal-sheet $F(\Jz,k)$ of
Equation~\eqref{eq:formfactor} or the Milky Way numbers of
Sections~\ref{sec:trapping} to \ref{sec:bias}. The arm scale is one number for all
radii and the wavenumber of a ring is fixed in the origin window; the exponents change
between baselines by less than the ring-to-ring scatter, so the 20 percent drift of
$k$ does not affect the result at this level. The perturbers stay in, so their direct
scattering is not separated by the control; the separation rests on the unchanged
$\JR$ of the migrators and on the near-Gaussian $\Delta\Lz$. The test uses one
realization of one disk model with a rigid halo over 2.98 Gyr, and its claims are
confined to 3 to 9 kpc. A perturber-free disk with self-excited arms remains to be
tested.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The provenance bias, the spiral $\JR^{\max}$--$\hZ$ relation, and the vertical
selectivity of corotation capture are tied together by a single analytic object, the
vertical form factor $F(k\hZ)$, which Equation~\eqref{eq:coupling} identifies as the
exact vertical Fourier coefficient of the spiral in the corotation-resonant
Hamiltonian. $F$ is the common thread, not the sole determinant of each magnitude:
the bias size additionally requires the migration weight set by the resonance regime
(Sections~\ref{sec:trapping}--\ref{sec:overlap}), while the
$\JR^{\max}$--$\hZ$ slope follows from the structural anisotropy factor
$\pi G\Sigma/\kappa\,(\sigR/\sigz)^2$; the form factor does not enter the slope
directly, its role being to select the cold-migrator population that defines
$\JR^{\max}$ and to share the same disk thickness $\alpha=k\hZ$.
Its radial twin is the classical \citet{DanielWyse2015} in-plane
selectivity; the $e^{-k|z|}$ reduction is standard \citep{BinneyTremaine2008}; and
the bias was found in simulations \citep{VeraCiro2016imprint,Mikkola2020}. What was
missing is the vertical extension that turns the verbal mechanism into
Equations~\eqref{eq:coupling}--\eqref{eq:wsqrtf} and connects it to
\citet{Palicio2024}. The form factor should not be conflated with the
finite-thickness reduction factors of classical density-wave theory
\citep{Toomre1964}, which weaken a self-gravitating disk's \emph{collective}
response to a density wave: $F$ is instead the orbit-average of an externally
imposed spiral felt by a \emph{single} star at fixed $\Jz$, a test-particle
coupling, not a modification of the self-gravitating response.

A complementary first-principles link between migration and vertical structure
already exists: \citet{Roskar2013} show, from vertical-action conservation, that an
outward migrator's scale height grows as $h_{Z,f}/h_{Z,i}=\exp(\Delta R/2R_d)$.
That law governs the \emph{fate} of a star once it has migrated; the form factor
governs the \emph{selectivity} of capture, which stars migrate at all. The two are
distinct and complementary, and only the latter produces the provenance bias.

The simulation of Section~\ref{sec:nbody} measures both halves and settles which
picture of the post-migration state this framework is consistent with.
\citet{SchonrichBinney2009} argued that outward migration contributes to disk
thickening because migrators keep their vertical velocity dispersion as they move to a
weaker restoring force, in the reading of \citet{Loebman2011} and \citet{Roskar2013},
and \citet{Loebman2011} invoked that picture for the thickening in their simulation
without comparing inward and outward migrators with the population at their
destination. \citet{Minchev2012thickening}, assuming instead the approximate
conservation of the vertical action, found that outward migrators cool and inward
migrators heat, so that their effect on the local disk must be assessed relative to
the destination population, and \citet{VeraCiro2014} found in the disk used here that
the vertical dispersion of outward migrators always decreases while that of inward
migrators increases; \citet{MinchevChiappiniMartig2014} showed that a massive merger
reverses the roles. In Section~\ref{sec:nbody} the capture bias is symmetric in
direction and the arrivals follow $\Jz$ conservation inside 9 kpc, so the framework
is consistent with \citet{Minchev2012thickening} and \citet{VeraCiro2014} and not with
the energy-conserving picture, and the fate law of \citet{Roskar2013} quoted above,
which assumes the conservation of $\Jz$, is the one to combine with it.

The two dynamical regimes the form factor distinguishes, single-resonance
trapping ($W\propto F^{1/2}$) and resonance-overlap diffusion ($W$ steeper), are
the two ends of one axis. The diffusive end is the inhomogeneous Balescu--Lenard
description of secular evolution in tightly wound disks \citep{Fouvry2015}, and the
linear-response perturbed distribution function of \citet{MonariFamaeySiebert2016}
is its complement; a full treatment would compute $W(\Jz)$ as the phase-space
trapped fraction across the overlap transition rather than in its two limits, which is
left to future work.

Several caveats bound the calculation. The finite-thickness softening of the
$e^{-k|z|}$ kernel (Section~\ref{sec:formfactor}, Equation~\eqref{eq:softened}) is an
order-unity correction at the Galactic $\alpha\sim1$, and Section~\ref{sec:nbody}
shows that it is not optional: with arms 0.8 as thick as the disk the razor-thin
kernel underestimates the exponent of the diffusion by a factor of 1.4, and the Milky
Way estimates above carry the thickness through $F_{\rm soft}$ with $h\sim0.5$ to 1
because the thickness of the Galactic arms is not measured in the same way. The
overlap parameter \eqref{eq:chirikov} is sensitive to the bar and spiral pattern
speeds; Table~\ref{tab:overlap} shows that the threshold conclusion holds across the
literature range of the bar, and a slower bar, a faster spiral or the Milky Way's
recurrent transient spirals only deepen the overlap. Finally, the size of the bias is
a property of the selection rule, not of the disk alone: the $N$-body disk gives 5 to
22 percent according to the rule, each reproduced by the matching weight in $F$, and
the comparison with \citet{VeraCiro2016imprint} and \citet{Mikkola2020}, who establish
the cold-migrator preference without a dispersion offset per rule, is at the level of
the regime ordering, single-resonance trapping near $-7$ percent against the $F^{3/2}$
to $F^2$ weights of the diffusive regime. The quantitative claims of this paper hold
inside 9 kpc and for baselines up to 2 Gyr. The age-running of the slope
\eqref{eq:agerun} is, to my knowledge, an untested and clean prediction for current
Gaia--APOGEE--LAMOST data.

\section{The softened (finite-thickness) form factor}
\label{app:softened}

A spiral arm is overdense disk material of finite vertical extent rather than a
razor-thin sheet. Model its vertical mass profile as a normalized exponential
$g(z)=(\beta/2)\,e^{-\beta|z|}$ with $\beta\equiv1/h_s$ ($\int\!g\,\mathrm{d}z=1$), and
keep the in-plane pattern $\Sigma_0 e^{ikx}$, so $\rho(x,z)=\Sigma_0 e^{ikx}\,g(z)$.
Writing $\Phi=e^{ikx}\psi(z)$ reduces Poisson's equation to
$\psi''-k^2\psi=4\pi G\Sigma_0\,g(z)$; the Green's function of $\partial_z^2-k^2$ is the
razor-thin kernel itself, $\mathcal{G}(z)=-e^{-k|z|}/2k$, so the potential is that
kernel convolved with the source,
%
\begin{equation}
  \psi(z)\;\propto\;\big(e^{-k|\cdot|}\!*g\big)(z)
  =\frac{\beta\big(k\,e^{-\beta|z|}-\beta\,e^{-k|z|}\big)}{k^2-\beta^2}.
  \label{eq:convolution}
\end{equation}
%
Normalizing to $K(0)=1$ gives the softened kernel
$K(z)=[\beta\,e^{-k|z|}-k\,e^{-\beta|z|}]/(\beta-k)$ used in
Section~\ref{sec:formfactor}. The orbit average is linear in the $z$-dependence, and
each exponential is itself a form factor at its own wavenumber,
$\langle e^{-k|z|}\rangle=F(\Jz,\alpha)$ and $\langle e^{-\beta|z|}\rangle=F(\Jz,1/h)$
with $h\equiv h_s/z_0$; hence $\langle K\rangle$ is Equation~\eqref{eq:softened}. It
recovers the razor-thin $F$ as $h\to0$ and tends to unity as $h\to\infty$, when the
star never escapes the arm. For the $\mathrm{sech}^2$ profile of the arms measured in
Section~\ref{sec:nbody}, $g(z)\propto\mathrm{sech}^2(z/z_a)$, the convolution
$\phi_k(z)\propto\int\mathrm{sech}^2(z'/z_a)\,e^{-k|z-z'|}\,\mathrm{d}z'$ has no
elementary closed form and is evaluated numerically; the layer form factor is its
orbit average, $\langle\phi_k(z)/\phi_k(0)\rangle$, flat for $|z|\lesssim z_a$ and
proportional to $e^{-k|z|}$ beyond.

\section{The exact trapped fraction}
\label{app:trapped}

The pendulum~\eqref{eq:pendulum} has separatrix
$p_{\rm sep}(\theta_r)=2\sqrt{\Psi/g}\,|\sin(\theta_r/2)|$; since $\Psi\propto F$
\eqref{eq:coupling}, $p_{\rm sep}=p_{\max}\sqrt{F}\,\sin(\theta_r/2)$ with
$p_{\max}=2\sqrt{\Psi_0/g}$ the $F{=}1$ half-width. For a parent population Gaussian in
the slow action, $N(p)\propto e^{-p^2/2\sigma_p^2}$, the fraction trapped at fixed
$\theta_r$ (those with $|p|<p_{\rm sep}$) is $\mathrm{erf}(p_{\rm sep}/\sqrt2\,\sigma_p)$.
Averaging over $\theta_r$ and folding to $u=\theta_r/2\in(0,\pi)$, with the single
island-width parameter $\xi\equiv p_{\max}(F{=}1)/\sigma_p\propto\sqrt{|\Phi_s|}$,
yields the exact trapped weight, Equation~\eqref{eq:wexact}. The narrow-island limit
($\xi\to0$, $\mathrm{erf}\,x\to2x/\sqrt\pi$) recovers the island-counting law
$W\propto\sqrt{F}$~\eqref{eq:wsqrtf}, while the wide-island limit ($\xi\to\infty$,
$\mathrm{erf}\to1$) gives $W\to1$ and washes out the selectivity.
