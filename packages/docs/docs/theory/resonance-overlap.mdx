# 4. Resonance overlap and the diffusive regime

**Goal of this section.** [Section 3](/theory/corotation-hamiltonian) showed that a
single corotation resonance floors the provenance bias near $-7$ percent, too weak to
match the simulations. Here we show that when several patterns are present, the bar and
the spiral plus the transient arms, their trapping islands overlap and the regular
pendulum trapping turns into stochastic, diffusive churning. Diffusion is steeper in
$F$ and deepens the bias into a derived band of $-20$ to $-27$ percent. We test with the
Chirikov criterion that the Milky Way sits at this transition. We find that the
transition is itself biased toward cold stars.

## The island width as a fraction of the radius

The trapping island of Section 3 has the half-width $\Delta p = 2\sqrt{\Psi/|g|}$ in the
slow action $p = L_z - L_{\rm CR}$. To compare islands of different patterns we need their
widths in radius. For a flat rotation curve, $\Omega = V_c/R$, the angular momentum of a
circular orbit is $L_z = RV_c$, so a shift in $L_z$ is a shift in radius,
$\Delta R = \Delta p/V_c$, while the curvature of the pendulum is

$$
g = \frac{\mathrm{d}\Omega}{\mathrm{d}L_z}
= \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}L_z}\!\left(\frac{V_c^2}{L_z}\right)
= -\frac{V_c^2}{L_z^2} = -\frac{1}{R^2} .
$$

Substituting $\Psi \simeq |\Phi_s|\,G\,F$ and $|g| = 1/R^2$ into
$\Delta R = 2\sqrt{\Psi/|g|}/V_c$ gives Equation (10) of the paper,

$$
\frac{\Delta R}{R} = 2\sqrt{\epsilon\,G\,F}, \qquad
\epsilon \equiv \frac{|\Phi_s|}{V_c^2} ,
$$

so the island is a fixed fraction of the corotation radius, set by the dimensionless
spiral strength $\epsilon$, typically 1 to 3 percent, together with the vertical
action of the star through $F$. The dependence $\Delta R \propto \sqrt{F}$ is the point to hold on to.

## The Chirikov criterion

Chirikov (1979) gave the rule for when isolated resonances merge into chaos: two
resonances overlap when the sum of their half-widths exceeds their separation. For an
inner pattern with corotation at $R_{\rm in}$ and an outer one at $R_{\rm out}$ we define
the overlap parameter

$$
S \equiv \frac{\Delta R_{\rm in} + \Delta R_{\rm out}}{R_{\rm out} - R_{\rm in}} ,
\qquad \text{overlap when } S \ge 1 .
$$

Writing the radii through the pattern speeds, $R = V_c/\Omega$, the numerator is
$2\sqrt{\epsilon GF}\,V_c\,(\Omega_{\rm in} + \Omega_{\rm out})/(\Omega_{\rm in}\Omega_{\rm out})$
and the denominator $V_c\,(\Omega_{\rm in} - \Omega_{\rm out})/(\Omega_{\rm in}\Omega_{\rm out})$,
so the common factor cancels, which leaves Equation (11) of the paper:

$$
S = 2\sqrt{\epsilon\,G\,F}\;
\frac{\Omega_{\rm in} + \Omega_{\rm out}}{\Omega_{\rm in} - \Omega_{\rm out}} \;\ge\; 1 .
$$

:::note Background: what overlap does to an orbit
The [primer](/primer/diffusion) integrates the simplest system with two resonances and
shows its stroboscopic map as the Chirikov parameter grows: below the threshold
invariant curves separate the two islands, near it the chaotic layers of the two
separatrices join, above it a star started at one resonance reaches the other. The
criterion is a rule of thumb, since the last invariant curve breaks at about two thirds
of the simple estimate, but it captures the transition to within that factor.
:::

## The Milky Way at the threshold

The two dominant patterns are the bar and the spiral. With the fiducial pattern speeds
$\Omega_p \approx 38$ and $20\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,kpc^{-1}}$, corotation at 6.0 and 11.5
kpc for $V_c = 230\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$, the pattern-speed factor is
$(38 + 20)/(38 - 20) = 3.22$, so for a midplane star with an order-unity radial factor
$S = 2\sqrt{\epsilon}\times 3.22$, that is

$$
S(\epsilon{=}0.01) = 0.64, \qquad S(\epsilon{=}0.02) = 0.91, \qquad S(\epsilon{=}0.03) = 1.12 .
$$

At the physical spiral strength the bar and spiral corotation resonances sit at the
overlap threshold, the mechanism of Minchev and Famaey (2010). Quillen (2003) first
applied the overlap argument to the Galactic bar and spiral, for the inner Lindblad
resonance of the spiral against the outer Lindblad resonance of the bar near the Sun,
a different pair from the corotation pair estimated here. The conclusion depends on the
two pattern speeds, for which the table gives $S$ at $\epsilon = 0.02$ and $F = 1$ across
the literature range.

$31 \pm 1$ when the far side of the bar is included, for which $S = 1.31$. For the
mainstream values of the bar, 33 to 41, $S$ runs from 1.13 to 0.82, so the Milky Way stays

## The transition is biased toward cold stars

Because $\Delta R \propto \sqrt{F}$, the overlap parameter inherits the vertical
dependence, $S \propto \sqrt{F}$: vertically cold stars have wider islands and cross the
threshold first. At the fiducial parameters the cold population, $F = 1$, overlaps at
$\epsilon = 0.024$, inside the physical band, whereas a hotter population with $F = 0.5$
stays in regular libration until $\epsilon = 0.048$, from
$2\sqrt{\epsilon F}\times 3.22 = 1$.

The diffusive channel is therefore itself biased toward cold stars, which steepens the
provenance bias beyond the single-resonance $F^{1/2}$ law toward the diffusive $F^2$ law
derived next. The Milky Way lives at a crossover that is selective in vertical action,
exactly the ingredient the single-resonance estimate lacks.

## One weight that spans both regimes

The two regimes are limits of a single migration weight. Without overlap, Section 3
gives $W \propto F^{1/2}$. With overlap, quasilinear churning gives an angular-momentum
diffusion coefficient $D \propto |\Psi|^2 \propto F^2$, the square of the coupling
([primer](/primer/diffusion)), so $W \propto F^2$. We model the exponent as running
smoothly from $1/2$ to $2$ through a gate centered on the threshold, Equation (12) of
the paper:

$$
W(J_z) = F^{\,p}, \qquad p = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{3}{2}\,\frac{S^2}{1 + S^2}, \qquad
S(J_z) = S_0\sqrt{F(J_z, k)} ,
$$

with the one physical parameter $S_0$, the effective overlap. The gate gives $p \to 1/2$
for $S \to 0$, pure trapping; $p \to 2$ for $S \to \infty$, pure diffusion; and
$p = 5/4$ at the threshold. The crossover happens at the critical form factor
$F_{\rm crit} = 1/S_0^2$, where $S = 1$: a critical vertical action separates the
diffusive, cold migrators from the confined, hot ones, a structural prediction. The
endpoints are physical; the gate is the minimal model of the sharpness of the
crossover.

## The diffusive endpoint is derived, not assumed

The $F^2$ ceiling follows from kinetic theory. The inhomogeneous Balescu-Lenard equation
describes the secular evolution of a self-gravitating disk by resonant relaxation, with
the angular-momentum diffusion coefficient

$$
D_{L_z}(J) = \pi\sum_m |\Psi_m(J)|^2\,C_m(\Omega)
$$

(Heyvaerts 2010; Fouvry, Pichon and Magorrian 2015), which inherits the
$|\Psi_m|^2 \propto F^2$ of the corotation coupling of Section 3, so that the bare
diffusive weight is $W \propto F^2$. One refinement: a trapped star librates with a
finite frequency width $\Delta\omega \propto \sqrt{\Psi} \propto \sqrt{F}$, which broadens
the resonance (Dupree 1966). For a Lorentzian spiral spectrum of width $W_s$ the
broadening gives the closed form of Equation (13) of the paper,

$$
W(J_z) = \frac{F^2}{1 + b\sqrt{F}}, \qquad b = \frac{\Delta\omega}{W_s} ,
$$

with the limits $W \to F^2$ for sharp resonances, $b \to 0$, the decorrelated
quasilinear limit, against $W \propto F^{3/2}$ for broad ones, $b \to \infty$, the coherent,
resonance-broadened limit.

_**A narrow, parameter-free band.** The provenance bias from the Balescu-Lenard weight

This is the strongest statement of the paper: the size of the provenance bias is a
near-parameter-free consequence of the disk being in the overlap regime. The crossover
weight bridges the derived $\sqrt{F}$ value and this derived diffusive band. The
mechanism is tested in [Section 6](/results/nbody-test), first by direct orbit
integration in an imposed spiral and then in a self-consistent $N$-body disk.

:::tip What to carry forward
The Milky Way sits at the Chirikov overlap threshold, $S \sim 1$, which cold stars
cross first. Overlap turns trapping, $W \propto F^{1/2}$, into diffusion,
$W \propto F^2$, whose bias is a near-parameter-free $-20$ to $-27$ percent.
[Section 5](/results/provenance-bias) turns this into the dispersion offset per
selection rule and into the slope of the $J_R^{\max}$-$h_Z$ relation.
:::
