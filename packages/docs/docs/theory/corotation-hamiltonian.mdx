# 3. The corotation-resonant Hamiltonian and the trapped weight

**Goal of this section.** This is the core of the argument. We show that the form factor
$F$ of [Section 2](/theory/form-factor) is not an inserted weight but the exact vertical
coupling of the spiral at corotation. We then reduce the resonance to a pendulum, read
off which stars are trapped and migrate, and compute the resulting provenance bias. The
outcome is that a single arm produces a bias of at most about $-7$ percent, which forces
the analysis into the overlap regime of [Section 4](/theory/resonance-overlap).

## The spiral in action-angle variables

We use the background actions $(J_R, L_z, J_z)$ and their angles
$(\theta_R, \theta_\phi, \theta_z)$, with frequencies $(\kappa, \Omega, \nu_z)$
([primer](/primer/orbits)). A steady, $m$-armed, tightly wound spiral of pattern speed
$\Omega_p$ is

$$
\Phi_s = \mathrm{Re}\!\left[\Phi_0(R)\,e^{-k|z|}\,
e^{\,i\left(m\phi + \int\! \mathrm{d}R\, k - m\Omega_p t\right)}\right] ,
$$

with $\Phi_0(R)$ the in-plane amplitude, $e^{-k|z|}$ the vertical fall-off of Section 2,
and a phase that describes an $m$-armed pattern winding through the disk and rotating
at $\Omega_p$. The position of a star is itself an oscillating function of its angles,
so we expand $\Phi_s$ as a Fourier series in the background angles, the natural basis
because the angles advance linearly in time:

$$
\Phi_s = \sum_{n_R,\,n_z} \Phi_{n_R n_z}(J_R, L_z, J_z)\,
e^{\,i\left(n_R\theta_R + m\theta_\phi + n_z\theta_z - m\Omega_p t\right)} .
$$

Here $n_R$ and $n_z$ are the integer harmonics of the radial and vertical angles, and the
azimuthal harmonic is fixed at the arm number $m$. Each coefficient is an average over
the fast angles $\theta_R$ and $\theta_z$, which wind through $2\pi$ at $\kappa$ and
$\nu_z$, tens of Myr per cycle, at fixed actions. The slow angle, the phase of the star
relative to the pattern, is not averaged away; it survives as the pendulum coordinate
$\theta_r$ below. Because the radial epicycle and the vertical oscillation are independent
motions, the radial and vertical averages separate.

## The resonant term

A term of the sum has the phase
$\psi_{n_R n_z} = n_R\theta_R + m\theta_\phi + n_z\theta_z - m\Omega_p t$, whose rate is
$\dot\psi = n_R\kappa + m\Omega + n_z\nu_z - m\Omega_p$. If the rate is not zero the term
oscillates and averages to nothing over time; it acts resonantly only when its phase is
stationary,

$$
n_R\kappa + n_z\nu_z + m(\Omega - \Omega_p) = 0 .
$$

Corotation is the case $\Omega = \Omega_p$, where the condition reduces to
$n_R\kappa + n_z\nu_z = 0$, whose only solution for generic frequencies is
$n_R = n_z = 0$. The corotation-resonant piece of the spiral is therefore the $(0, 0)$
harmonic, the part of the potential averaged over both the radial epicycle and the
vertical orbit. This is why the orbit average of Section 2 is the right object: the
resonance keeps only the doubly averaged piece and discards every other harmonic, so
$F$ is precisely what the resonance responds to.

## The coupling factorizes

The resonant coupling $\Psi$ is the amplitude of the $(0, 0)$ term, the average of
$\Phi_s$ over the two fast angles at fixed actions,

$$
\Psi = \big\langle \Phi_s\big\rangle_{\theta_R,\theta_z}
= \frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}\int_0^{2\pi}\!\mathrm{d}\theta_R\int_0^{2\pi}\!\mathrm{d}\theta_z\;
\Phi_s .
$$

Radially the star sits at $R = R_{\rm CR} + a_R\cos\theta_R$ with the epicyclic amplitude
$a_R = \sqrt{2J_R/\kappa}$, so the radial phase of the spiral is
$\int\mathrm{d}R\,k \approx kR_{\rm CR} + k a_R\cos\theta_R$; vertically it sits at
$z(\theta_z; J_z)$, where the amplitude carries $e^{-k|z|}$. Pulling out the slowly
varying amplitude $\Phi_0(R_{\rm CR})$ and the slow phase, held fixed during the average,
the integrand becomes a radial factor times a vertical factor,

$$
\Psi = \Phi_0(R_{\rm CR})\;
\big\langle e^{ik a_R\cos\theta_R}\big\rangle_{\theta_R}\;
\big\langle e^{-k|z(\theta_z)|}\big\rangle_{\theta_z} .
$$

The radial average is the integral representation of the Bessel function,
$\langle e^{ik a_R\cos\theta_R}\rangle = J_0(k a_R) \equiv G(J_R)$, the classical radial
reduction factor (Binney and Tremaine 2008): a star with a large epicycle smears over
many wavelengths of the arm and couples weakly. The vertical average is the form factor
of Section 2. The amplitude of the resonant coupling at corotation is therefore

$$
\Psi(J_R, J_z) = \Phi_0(R_{\rm CR})\,G(J_R)\,F(J_z, k),
\qquad G(J_R) = J_0(k a_R), \quad a_R = \sqrt{2J_R/\kappa} ,
$$

Equation (5) of the paper. The complex phase of $\Phi_s$ does not depend on the fast
angles and passes through the average untouched, collapsing to $e^{i\theta_r}$ in the
slow angle, so the surviving term of the potential is $\Psi\cos\theta_r$. This is the
central identification of the paper: $F$ is the exact vertical Fourier coefficient of
the spiral at corotation, and the provenance bias is the $F$ factor of the corotation
coupling, as the in-plane selectivity of Daniel and Wyse (2015) is its $G$ factor.

## The resonance is a pendulum

In the frame rotating with the pattern the conserved energy is the Jacobi integral
$H_J = H_0 - \Omega_p L_z + \Phi_s$, with $H_0$ the unperturbed Hamiltonian. Averaging
over the fast angles, for which $J_R$ and $J_z$ are adiabatic invariants on the time
scale of the slow dynamics, leaves a Hamiltonian in $L_z$ and $\theta_r$ alone. The
invariance is approximate for an individual star in a time-dependent disk and holds in
the ensemble sense: Minchev et al. (2012) found the vertical action conserved on average
as stellar samples change guiding radius, Vera-Ciro and D'Onghia (2016) measured in a
$5\times10^6$-particle realization of the disk of [Section 6](/results/nbody-test) a
median fractional change below 1 percent over 40 orbital periods with a dispersion of 20
percent at 2 Gyr, and the migrators of Section 6 change their $J_z$ by 0 to 16 percent in
the median over 2 Gyr. Expanding the axisymmetric part $\bar H_0(L_z) \equiv H_0 - \Omega_p L_z$
about corotation in the offset $p \equiv L_z - L_{\rm CR}$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar H(p,\theta_r)
  &= \bar H_0(L_{\rm CR})
     + \frac{\mathrm{d}\bar H_0}{\mathrm{d}L_z}\bigg|_{\rm CR} p
     + \frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}^2\bar H_0}{\mathrm{d}L_z^2}\bigg|_{\rm CR} p^2
     + \cdots + \Psi\cos\theta_r \\[4pt]
  &= \text{const}
     + \underbrace{(\Omega-\Omega_p)\big|_{\rm CR}}_{=\,0}\, p
     + \frac{1}{2}\underbrace{\frac{\mathrm{d}\Omega}{\mathrm{d}L_z}\bigg|_{\rm CR}}_{\equiv\, g}\, p^2
     + \Psi\cos\theta_r ,
\end{aligned}
$$

where the linear term vanishes because $\mathrm{d}H_0/\mathrm{d}L_z = \Omega$ equals
$\Omega_p$ at corotation. Dropping the constant leaves the pendulum Hamiltonian of
Equation (6), the resonant-trapping construction of Monari et al. (2017) carrying $J_z$
through $\Psi$:

$$
\bar H(p,\theta_r) = \frac{1}{2}\,g\,p^2 + \Psi\cos\theta_r,
\qquad g \equiv \frac{\mathrm{d}\Omega}{\mathrm{d}L_z}\bigg|_{\rm CR} .
$$

The depth of the well is the coupling $\Psi$, and through $\Psi$ it is set by $F$.

:::note Background: the pendulum
The [primer](/primer/resonance) derives the same Hamiltonian from the motion in the
frame of the arm, draws its phase portrait, and maps the two kinds of motion back onto
the disk as horseshoe orbits. For a flat or declining rotation curve $g$ is negative,
so the stable point of the pendulum sits at $\theta_r = 0$, the inter-arm region of the
corotation circle, and the unstable one on the arm at $\theta_r = \pi$; the paper's
Appendix B writes the separatrix after the shift $\theta_r \to \theta_r + \pi$, which
places the island at $\pi$. The island width below does not depend on the choice.
:::

## The trapping island is $\propto\sqrt{F}$

A pendulum has two kinds of motion, circulation, a star that drifts past corotation,
and libration, a star trapped at corotation that is carried along with the arm and
migrates, separated by the separatrix through the unstable point. With $g < 0$ the
unstable point is $\theta_r = \pi$, at the energy $\bar H = -\Psi$, and the separatrix is
the level curve through it,

$$
\frac{1}{2}\,g\,p^2 + \Psi\cos\theta_r = -\Psi
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
p_{\rm sep}(\theta_r) = 2\sqrt{\frac{\Psi}{|g|}}\;\left|\cos\frac{\theta_r}{2}\right| ,
$$

widest at the stable point. The half-width of the trapping island in $p$ is therefore

$$
\Delta p = 2\sqrt{\Psi/|g|} \;\propto\; \sqrt{F(J_z, k)} ,
$$

because $\Psi \propto F$. This is the key inequality of the mechanism: vertically cold
stars, with large $F$, sit in wider trapping islands and are captured, and migrate,
preferentially.

The weight follows from counting. The parent stars have a smooth distribution $N(p)$ in
the slow action, centered on the corotation circle, and the island captures those that
fall inside it, a range of $p$ of order its width around $p = 0$. If the island is narrow
compared with the spread $\sigma_p$ of the parent distribution, $N(p)$ barely varies
across it, so the trapped number is the density times the width,
$N_{\rm trap} \approx N(0)\,\Delta p$, and the migration weight is

$$
W(J_z) \propto \Delta p \propto F(J_z, k)^{1/2} ,
$$

the island-area counting law, Equation (8) of the paper. It already biases migration toward cold stars, but only as $\sqrt{F}$, a gentle slope.
Its one assumption, that $N(p)$ is uniform across the island, is what the exact census
below drops.

## The exact trapped fraction

Model the parent population as a Gaussian in $p$ of dispersion $\sigma_p$,
$N(p) \propto e^{-p^2/2\sigma_p^2}$. At a fixed angle $\theta_r$ a star is trapped if
$|p| < p_{\rm sep}(\theta_r)$, and the fraction of the Gaussian inside that band is
$\mathrm{erf}(p_{\rm sep}/\sqrt{2}\,\sigma_p)$. Writing
$p_{\rm sep} = p_{\max}\sqrt{F}\,|\cos(\theta_r/2)|$ with $p_{\max} = 2\sqrt{\Psi_0/|g|}$ the
half-width of a midplane orbit, defining the island-width parameter

$$
\kappa \equiv \frac{p_{\max}(F{=}1)}{\sigma_p} \;\propto\; \sqrt{|\Phi_s|} ,
$$

and averaging over the angle, with the substitution $u = \theta_r/2$ folding the range
to $(0, \pi)$, gives the exact trapped weight, Equation (9) of the paper:

$$
W(J_z) = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\pi \mathrm{d}u\;
\mathrm{erf}\!\left(\frac{\kappa\sqrt{F(J_z, k)}}{\sqrt2}\,\sin u\right) .
$$

Its two limits can be read off the error function. For narrow islands, $\kappa \to 0$,
$\mathrm{erf}(x) \approx 2x/\sqrt{\pi}$ and the integrand is linear in $\sqrt{F}$, so
$W \propto \sqrt{F}$ and the counting law is recovered. For wide islands,
$\kappa \to \infty$, the error function saturates and $W \to 1$ for every star: the
weighting flattens and the bias is washed out.

## From weight to bias: the $-7$ percent floor

The equilibrium vertical distribution of an isothermal population is
$\mathrm{d}N/\mathrm{d}E_z \propto e^{-E_z}/\nu_z$, a Boltzmann factor times the density
of states, proportional to the period. The migrators are the same distribution
reweighted by $W(J_z)$. Since the vertical energy measures the squared vertical
excursion, the squared dispersions are the mean energies and the migrator-to-parent
dispersion ratio is

$$
\frac{\sigma_z^{\rm mig}}{\sigma_z^{\rm all}}
= \sqrt{\frac{\langle E_z\rangle_{\rm mig}}{\langle E_z\rangle_{\rm all}}}
= \sqrt{\frac{\int \mathrm{d}E_z\; E_z\,W\,(e^{-E_z}/\nu_z)}{\int \mathrm{d}E_z\; W\,(e^{-E_z}/\nu_z)}\Big/
\frac{\int \mathrm{d}E_z\; E_z\,(e^{-E_z}/\nu_z)}{\int \mathrm{d}E_z\; (e^{-E_z}/\nu_z)}} .
$$

The provenance bias is the log dispersion ratio $\ln\sigma_z^{\rm mig}/\sigma_z^{\rm all}$,
the quantity every figure plots. It is negative because $W$ favors small $E_z$, and the
more negative the stronger the bias; $-0.07$ means that the migrators are about 7
percent colder. It is a population-integrated number, the whole distribution
reweighted, not the value of $W$ or $F$ for any single star.

_**The single-resonance floor.** The provenance bias from the exact trapped weight at
the Milky Way thickness against the island width $\kappa$. It is most negative in the

percent for any spiral strength._

The conclusion is strong and slightly counterintuitive. Making the spiral stronger,
which raises $\kappa$, does not strengthen the single-resonance bias but weakens it,
because wide islands trap hot and cold stars alike. The most negative value the bias
can take, its floor, is the dilute value with $W \propto \sqrt{F}$, and at the Milky Way
thickness that floor is $-7$ percent.

:::caution The $-7$ percent is the log dispersion ratio, not a value of $\sqrt{F}$
Across the population $\sqrt{F}$ runs over roughly 0.3 to 1. The $-7$ percent is the
outcome of the reweighting integral: the weight $\sqrt{F}$ pulls the mean vertical
energy of the migrators down from $\langle E_z\rangle_{\rm all} = 1.11$ to
$\langle E_z\rangle_{\rm mig} = 0.96$, so the dispersion ratio is
$\sqrt{0.96/1.11} = 0.93$ and the bias is $\ln 0.93 = -0.07$. The
[interactive reweighting](/results/provenance-bias) of Section 5 shows the two
distributions and their means for every weight of the paper.
:::

Finite thickness softens the floor further. Replacing the razor-thin $F$ by the
$F_{\rm soft}$ of [Section 2](/theory/form-factor), the vertical extent of the arm makes
its gravity reach higher above the plane, so even hot stars feel an appreciable
amplitude and the contrast between hot and cold shrinks.

## Why a single resonance is not enough

Even the steeper capture-fraction accounting of Daniel and Wyse (2018), $W \propto F$
rather than $F^{1/2}$, reaches only about $-14$ percent. None of the single-resonance
estimates produces the strong cold-migrator preference of the simulations, in which the
migrators are drawn overwhelmingly from the kinematically cool population (Vera-Ciro,
D'Onghia and Navarro 2016). The arithmetic indicates that the Milky Way is not in the
clean single-resonance regime but in the regime where several resonances overlap and
trapping gives way to diffusive churning, which is steeper in $F$. That is the subject of
[Section 4](/theory/resonance-overlap).

:::tip What to carry forward
The pendulum width $\Delta p \propto \sqrt{F}$ is the engine of the provenance bias. A
single resonance floors the bias near $-7$ percent, weaker still with finite arm
thickness. The stronger preference of the simulations requires resonance overlap, which
steepens the weight from $F^{1/2}$ toward $F^2$ and deepens the bias to the derived
$-20$ to $-27$ percent band.
:::
