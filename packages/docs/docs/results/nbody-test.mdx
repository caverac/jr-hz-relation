# 6. The test in a self-consistent N-body disk

**Goal of this section.** The predictions of Sections 3 to 5 are tested in two steps.
First by direct orbit integration in an imposed spiral, which checks the mechanism with
no analytic input but shares the assumptions of the model, a fixed background, an
imposed spiral and a razor-thin arm. Then in a simulation that shares none of them, the
LD ("light disk") model of Vera-Ciro, D'Onghia and Navarro (2016), the disk introduced
by D'Onghia, Vogelsberger and Hernquist (2013) and analyzed by Vera-Ciro et al. (2014),
in its $10^8$-particle realization with recurrent multi-arm spirals, where the form
factor is measured on the simulation's own orbits instead of modeled. The outcome: the
variance of the angular-momentum change follows the form factor squared, the
dispersion of the migrators follows the selection rule, capture is symmetric in
direction, and the arrivals keep their vertical action. One correction comes out of the
test: the arms have a vertical thickness of their own, and the form factor must use the
potential of a thick arm rather than the razor-thin $e^{-k|z|}$. The commands that
reproduce every number are listed in [Using the library](/library/usage).

## Test particles

We integrate about 3000 near-circular orbits whose guiding radii are spread around
corotation, at $R \approx 1$ in units $R_0 = V_0 = 1$, each with a vertical velocity
dispersion $\sigma_z$, in the Galactic potential `MWPotential2014` of Bovy (2015), together
with one imposed transient spiral, a two-armed arm of pitch angle 15 degrees switched on
and off by a Gaussian envelope in time. The orbits run for a few orbital periods, and
each star is weighted by how much angular momentum it churned, $|\Delta L_z|$, the
numerical counterpart of the migration weight $W$. The vertical dispersion of the
weighted migrators is compared with that of the whole population, and the log of the
ratio is the bias plotted below.

The experiment is controlled by a few parameters, each tied to a quantity of the model:

| symbol | in the simulation | in the model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $z_0$ | the vertical scale height of the disk, $z_0 = \sigma_z/\nu_z(R{=}1) \approx 0.04$, about 0.4 kpc | the isothermal-sheet scale $z_0$ |
| $k$ | the radial wavenumber of the spiral, set by the arm number $m = 2$ and the pitch angle $i$, $k = m/(R\tan i) \approx 7.5$, held fixed | the wavenumber of $\Phi_s \propto e^{ikR}$ |
| $H$ | the vertical scale height of the spiral, the knob swept in the left panel | the vertical reach $1/k$ of the $e^{-k\lvert z\rvert}$ fall-off |
| $M$ | the number of overlapping patterns, distinct transients of different pattern speed with the same $m$ and $k$, the knob swept in the right panel | no single-number counterpart: $M$ raises the effective overlap $S$ through the spacing and widths of the resonances |

In the analytic model one number does double duty: $k$ sets the radial winding and,
through Laplace's equation, the vertical fall-off, so the vertical reach of the spiral
is locked to $1/k$. The simulation separates the two, with $k$ pinned by the geometry of
the arm and the vertical extent $H$ dialed independently, which lets us vary the
vertical thinness on its own and watch the form factor respond. The controlled knob is
the dimensionless thinness $\alpha \equiv z_0/H$, the counterpart of the analytic
$\alpha = k z_0$ with the model's $1/k$ replaced by $H$; the correspondence is only
approximate, since the vertical profile of the galpy spiral is a $\mathrm{sech}$-type
form rather than a pure exponential, although the ratio $z_0/H$ itself is exact. We
sweep $\alpha$ from 0.1 to 1 by setting $H = z_0/\alpha$; the Galactic point is
$\alpha \sim 1$, matching the analytic Milky Way value of 0.84.

```shell
uv run experiments test-particle-plot
```

The migrators are vertically colder, and the bias strengthens with $\alpha$ in agreement
with the single-resonance prediction, reaching $\ln\sigma_z^{\rm mig}/\sigma_z^{\rm all}
\approx -0.06$ near the Galactic $\alpha \sim 1$. The solid curve is the dilute $\sqrt{F}$
floor, the strongest bias a single resonance can produce, so points at or just below it
are the expected behavior at finite island width rather than a discrepancy. The right
panel carries no analytic curve because the overlap regime is governed by the effective
overlap $S$, set by the spacing and widths of the resonances and not by the bare count
$M$; it is a trend test, and the trend is the one the diffusive argument requires. The
$N$-body rings lie between the single-resonance curve and the razor-thin diffusive
curve, and on the diffusive curve of the thick arm, whose thickness is the one measured
below: the self-consistent disk sits where the diffusive regime with a thick arm puts
it, with no parameter adjusted.

## What the model predicts for the N-body disk

The robust statement of the paper is the corotation coupling $\Psi \propto F(J_z, k)$ of
[Section 3](/theory/corotation-hamiltonian). In the diffusive regime of
[Section 4](/theory/resonance-overlap) it gives $D_{L_z} \propto F^2$, so a star that
diffuses in $L_z$ for a time $\Delta t$ has a change $\Delta L_z$ whose root mean square is
proportional to $F$. Four things follow, and the test checks each of them.

1. In a ring of origin radius, $\mathrm{Var}(\Delta L_z)$ against $F$ is a power law of
   exponent 2, with no free parameter.
2. The vertical dispersion of the migrators relative to the whole ring,
   $\sigma_{z,\rm mig}/\sigma_{z,\rm all}$, depends on how migrators are selected. The
   weight of a star in the migrator population is $F$ when migrators are weighted by
   $\lvert\Delta L_z\rvert$, $F^2$ when weighted by $(\Delta L_z)^2$, and
   $\mathrm{erfc}(\lambda/F)$ with $\lambda = L_*/\sqrt{2V_1}$ when they are the stars
   with $\lvert\Delta L_z\rvert > L_*$, where $V_1$ is the variance of $\Delta L_z$ at
   $F = 1$. The model ratio for each rule is the ratio obtained by reweighting the same
   stars with that weight.
3. The weight depends on $J_z$ only, so inward and outward migrators of one origin ring
   have the same $J_z$ distribution.
4. A migrator keeps its vertical action, so at the destination its
   $\langle v_z^2\rangle$ is $J_z \nu_z$ with the local vertical frequency. The
   alternative, a migrator that keeps its vertical energy, predicts a different
   dispersion, and the simulation decides between the two.

## The simulation

| Property | Value |
| --- | --- |
| disk | $10^8$ particles of $181\,M_\odot$; $M_d = 1.81 \times 10^{10}\,M_\odot$, $R_d = 3.13$ kpc, $\mathrm{sech}^2$ profile with $z_0 = 0.313$ kpc |
| halo | rigid Hernquist, $9.5 \times 10^{11}\,M_\odot$, $a = 29.8$ kpc |
| perturbers | 1,000 particles of $9.5 \times 10^5\,M_\odot$ on circular orbits, in for the whole run |
| output | 1,020 snapshots to $t = 3.05$ in units of 0.9778 Gyr, every 4.9 Myr after $t = 0.51$ |
| control | the same disk without perturbers, 18 snapshots to $t = 0.85$ |
| other realizations | the perturbers removed after 245 Myr and 5.5 Gyr of evolution (Vera-Ciro et al. 2014); $5 \times 10^6$ particles for 6 Gyr (Vera-Ciro and D'Onghia 2016) and 9.8 Gyr (Vera-Ciro, D'Onghia and Navarro 2016) |

The perturbers seed the arms, which then grow by swing amplification (D'Onghia et al.
2013). The spiral is a set of local corotating features rather than one global mode:
in the origin window of the test, $t = 0.5$ to 0.8, the harmonics $m = 5$ to 8 dominate
with density amplitudes of 9 to 13 percent at 5 to 8 kpc, pitch angles of 20 to 40
degrees and radial wavenumbers of 1.4 to 2.5 kpc$^{-1}$, and each harmonic shows several
spectrogram peaks whose corotation lies within 1 kpc of the peak radius. The amplitudes
decline slowly, to 7 to 10 percent at $t = 1.5$ to 1.8 and 5 to 8 percent at $t = 2.5$
to 2.8, while the combined wavenumber drifts by 20 percent on average.

```shell
uv run experiments nbody-spiral staging/LD --windows 0.5:0.8,1.5:1.8,2.5:2.8
```

The snapshots are read once by `nbody-extract`, which keeps a random $10^6$ disk
particles as time series (the tracks) and, per snapshot, sums over all $10^8$ particles
in 80 rings of 0.25 kpc (counts, $\sum\cos m\phi$ and $\sum\sin m\phi$ for $m = 1$ to 8,
and the moments of $z$, $v_R$, $v_\phi$ and $v_z$). The products take 15 GB and stay
outside the repository; the tables of the test are cached under `assets/data/nbody/`.

## The form factor is measured along the orbits

`nbody-window` summarizes every tracked particle over a window of 293 Myr (60
snapshots): the mean $L_z$ and radius, the actions $J_z = \frac{1}{2\pi}\oint v_z\,dz$
and $J_R = \frac{1}{2\pi}\oint v_R\,dR$ as loop integrals between the first and the last
upward crossing, the periods from the same crossings, and the form factor
$F = \langle\phi_k(z)/\phi_k(0)\rangle$ along the orbit for wavenumbers from 0.5 to 4
kpc$^{-1}$. The loop integrals recover synthetic orbits sampled at the run's cadence to
0.1 percent. In the origin window 85 percent of the tracked stars complete a vertical
period (median $T_z = 82$ Myr) and 72 percent a radial one (median $T_R = 97$ Myr); the
rest are in the outer disk.

```shell
uv run experiments nbody-window staging/LD --start 0.5
uv run experiments nbody-window staging/LD --start 1.0
```

Each disk star gets an origin ring from the guiding radius of its mean $L_z$ on the
model rotation curve, and $\Delta L_z$ is the change of its window-mean $L_z$ to a later
window. The three baselines are 0.49, 0.98 and 1.96 Gyr, from the origin window to
$t = 1.0$, 1.5 and 2.5. The form factor of a ring is evaluated at the ring's wavenumber,
the mean over $m = 5$ to 8 of each harmonic's wavenumber with the squared amplitudes as
weights.

The exponent of $\mathrm{Var}(\Delta L_z) \propto F^b$ is fitted by weighted least squares

### The arms are thick, and the razor-thin form factor fails inside 8 kpc

With $F = \langle e^{-k|z|}\rangle$, the exponents inside 8 kpc are 0.9 to 1.35 (the
circles of the figure), which the decision rule of the plan would have read as the
crossover regime of [Section 4](/theory/resonance-overlap). A sweep of the wavenumber of
$F$ over the stored grid explains the pattern: in each ring the product $b_k k$ is
nearly constant while $b_k$ itself falls by a factor of 6 from $k = 0.5$ to 4
kpc$^{-1}$. The variance therefore follows $\exp(-2k_{\rm eff}\langle|z|\rangle)$ with
one effective wavenumber per ring, and that $k_{\rm eff}$ is about half the arm
wavenumber inside 8 kpc. The local slopes also flatten toward high $F$, that is, at small
$|z|$. Both are what the potential of a thick arm produces: it is flat for $|z|$ below the
arm's vertical scale and decays as $e^{-k|z|}$ only outside it.

```shell
uv run experiments nbody-migration ... --form thin --harmonics 7 --k-sweep
uv run experiments nbody-arms staging/LD --start 0.5 --length 0.3
```

`nbody-arms` measures the arms' vertical profile from the tracks: in rings of radius,
the Fourier amplitude of the particles in bins of $|z|$ is projected on the phase of the
whole ring. For $m = 7$ at 5 to 8 kpc the relative amplitude is 11 to 12 percent at
$|z| < 0.1$ kpc, 8.6 to 9.0 percent at 0.3 to 0.45 kpc, 4.8 to 7.0 percent at 0.45 to
0.65 kpc and 2.2 to 4.0 percent at 0.65 to 1 kpc, with the arm at height lagging the
midplane arm by up to 50 degrees. The implied scale of a $\mathrm{sech}^2$ layer with the
arm's second moment is $z_a = 0.24$ to 0.29 kpc for every harmonic inside 10 kpc, 0.77 to
0.93 of the disk's $z_0 = 0.313$ kpc, and 0.25 to 0.26 kpc for the strongest harmonic.

The correction is the one derived in [Section 2](/theory/form-factor), with the arm's
measured profile in place of the exponential: the in-plane Fourier component of the
potential of an arm whose density follows $\mathrm{sech}^2(z/z_a)$ is

$$
\phi_k(z) \propto \int \mathrm{sech}^2\!\left(\frac{z'}{z_a}\right) e^{-k|z - z'|}\,\mathrm{d}z' ,
$$

and the layer form factor is $F = \langle\phi_k(z)/\phi_k(0)\rangle$ along the orbit. It
is flat for $|z| \lesssim z_a$ and decays as $e^{-k|z|}$ outside, and it tends to the
razor-thin $F$ as $z_a \to 0$. `nbody-window` stores both factors (`form` and
`form_layer`, with `--arm-scale` defaulting to the measured 0.25 kpc), and
`nbody-migration --form layer` is the default. With it the exponents move to the values
quoted above. A scan of the arm scale confirms the choice: at the arm wavenumber a layer
as thick as the disk gives 1.5 to 2.2 inside 8 kpc with local slopes near 2 across the
whole range of $F$, whereas outside 8 kpc a thinner layer of 0.1 to 0.2 kpc gives 2 and
the disk's thickness gives 2.5 to 3.4.

The misfit ranges at 0.49 and 0.98 Gyr are the same within a point. The model is
slightly too strong inside 8 kpc and slightly too weak at 8 to 10 kpc, following the
exponent of Result 1. The ordering of the rules is the predicted one in every ring: the
threshold selects the coldest stars, the linear weight the least cold. The bias in this
disk is therefore 5 to 9 percent, 10 to 16 percent or 16 to 22 percent depending on how
migrators are defined, which is why the paper quotes the offset per rule rather than as
one number. The same rings appear in the test-particle figure above, placed at the
thinness $\alpha = k z_0$ of each ring next to the test particles and the analytic
weights.

## Result 3: capture is symmetric, and arrivals keep $J_z$

In every origin ring the inward and the outward migrators have the same $J_z$
distribution: the Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance between them is 0.02 to 0.12 in the three
baselines, and both groups have 0.55 to 0.8 of the ring's median $J_z$. The innermost
ring sends most of its migrators outward and the outermost inward, which is the mean
drift of the disk (the mean $\Delta L_z$ is $+25$ kpc km/s at 3 to 4 kpc and $-26$ at
11 to 12 kpc after 1.96 Gyr) and not an asymmetry of the capture.

<Figure src="/figures/nbody-destination.png" alt="Vertical dispersion of the arrivals relative to the non-migrators of the destination ring, inward and outward, with the two predictions" />

_**The destination state** at the 1.96 Gyr baseline. The vertical dispersion of the
arrivals in each ring relative to the stars that stayed there (points), for inward
(top) and outward (bottom) migrators, against the prediction with $J_z$ conserved
(solid) and with $\langle v_z^2\rangle$ conserved (dashed)._

Inside 9 kpc the $J_z$-conserving prediction is within 1.5 to 7 points of the
measurement both ways, while the energy-conserving one is off by 11 to 15 points and
has the wrong sign for outward migrators, which it makes hotter than the locals. Inward
migrators arrive 24 to 29 percent colder than the locals and outward migrators 6 to 12
percent colder, as the estimate $e^{\Delta R/4R_d}$ times the provenance ratio gives for
a constant-$z_0$ disk. This is the picture of Minchev et al. (2012) and of Vera-Ciro et
al. (2014), not the energy-conserving one of Schoenrich and Binney (2009). Outside 9 kpc
the arrivals are hotter than the $J_z$ prediction by a gap that grows with the baseline,
6 to 11 points at 0.49 Gyr and 8 to 15 at 1.96 Gyr, as vertical heating along the way
would produce; the quantitative claims of the paper stop at 9 kpc for this reason.

## What else the simulation shows

The perturbers are not the direct cause. `nbody-floor` compares the same particles in
the control run: between $t = 0.5$ and 0.8 the disk's own variance of $\Delta L_z$ is 0.4
to 0.8 percent of the run's in every ring. The perturbers stay in for the whole run, so
their direct scattering is not separated by a control. What separates it from
corotation churning is the radial action: inside 8 kpc the migrators' median relative
change of $J_R$ is 0.22 to 0.51 against 0.29 to 0.53 for the rest of the same rings, and
the distribution of $\Delta L_z$ has an excess kurtosis of 0.2 to 1.7 inside 10 kpc.
Scattering would heat the migrators radially everywhere and leave a tail; churning does
neither.

```shell
uv run experiments nbody-floor staging/LD-control staging/LD --times 0.5,0.8
```

The result does not depend on the drift of the arms. The wavenumber is set once, in
the origin window, and drifts by 20 percent on average (up to 40 percent at single radii)
between the three test windows. The exponents measured against the origin-window $F$
change between the baselines by less than the ring-to-ring scatter.

## What the test does not show

- The form factor is measured along the orbits, not modeled: the test checks the
  exponent and the reweighting, not the isothermal-sheet $F(J_z, k)$ of Section 2.
- The arm scale is one number, 0.25 kpc, for all radii, and the wavenumber of a ring is
  one number for the whole baseline.
- Outside 9 kpc the arrivals do not follow $J_z$ conservation and the exponents are above
  2.
- One realization, one disk model, a rigid halo, 2.98 Gyr; the published runs of the
  same disk removed the perturbers at 245 Myr.
- The biases of 5 to 22 percent depend on the rule; the $-20$ to $-41$ percent band of
  the Boltzmann weight of [Section 5](/results/provenance-bias) is not reproduced as such.

## Reproducing the numbers

The whole chain runs with the `nbody-*` commands of the library, from the GADGET
snapshots on an external drive to the figures of this page. The extraction products
live in the `staging/` folder of the repository, which is not committed (15 GB); the
cached tables under `assets/data/nbody/` let `nbody-plot`, `figures` and the interactive
figure above redraw the results without them. The extraction takes about 10 hours for
the 611 snapshots; every later step takes seconds to a minute. The commands, with their
inputs and outputs, are tabulated in [Using the library](/library/usage).

:::tip What to carry forward
In a self-consistent disk the variance of the angular-momentum change grows as
$F^{1.9}$ to $F^{2.0}$ over 0.5 to 2 Gyr, the bias of the migrators is reproduced per
selection rule by the matching weight in $F$ to within 5 points, capture is symmetric
in direction, and inside 9 kpc the arrivals keep $J_z$. The form factor that does this
is the orbit average of the potential of a thick arm, with the arm's measured scale of
0.8 disk scale heights; the razor-thin $e^{-k|z|}$ underestimates the exponent by a
factor of 1.4. The [Discussion](/discussion) places the result against the thickening
literature and lists what remains untested.
:::
